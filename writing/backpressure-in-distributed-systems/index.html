Backpressure in Distributed Systems: Stability, Correctness, and Graceful Degradation

This article explains how admission control, bounded queues, and adaptive concurrency keep distributed systems stable under burst traffic and partial failure.

Why this matters in production

Summary: Backpressure is the control surface that prevents overload from turning partial failure into systemic failure.

When request intake, queue depth, and worker concurrency are unconstrained, reliability drops even before hard outages. This model is used repeatedly across the site’s migration and control-plane material.
