How I Think About Failure

  • Systems fail under load, not only from code defects.
  • Unbounded queues and retry storms can amplify partial failures.
  • Backpressure, admission control, and load shedding protect system stability.
  • Design for graceful degradation instead of idealized perfect uptime.

Systems don’t fail all at once — they fail under load. Backpressure is how you stay in control.

What this is

A production notification platform migration from legacy infrastructure to OCI-native service patterns.

Why this mattered

Migration quality directly influenced customer confidence and enterprise delivery readiness.

Core problem

Legacy pathways constrained elasticity, supportability, and controlled rollout operations.

My role

Led migration execution and backend implementation planning across the service transition.

Key engineering decisions

  • Controlled traffic shifts instead of all-at-once cutover
  • Queueing and worker isolation to reduce overload amplification
  • Observability-first rollout with explicit metrics checkpoints
  • See: Backpressure in Distributed Systems: Stability, Correctness, and Graceful Degradation

Tradeoffs

Balanced migration speed against operational safety and support continuity.
