Redis vs. ZooKeeper vs. etcd for Distributed Locking

There is no universal winner. The right tool depends on whether lock failure is an efficiency issue or a correctness incident.

Start here: Distributed lock overview and failure framing.

Coordination model differences

Redis: key-value primitive often used for lock/lease behavior with compare-and-delete unlock scripts. Very common operationally, low latency, strong for efficiency-focused coordination.

ZooKeeper: coordination service with ephemeral and sequential znodes, watches, and recipe-driven lock/election workflows.

etcd: consensus-backed KV with leases and transactional comparisons; explicit fit for linearizable coordination contracts.

Guarantees and risks

  • Release semantics: always owner-checked release; never blind delete.
  • Stale-owner risk: present in all systems unless write path validates ownership version/token.
  • Latency: Redis often lowest median; etcd/ZooKeeper add consensus/session overhead for stronger semantics.
  • Operational complexity: ZooKeeper and etcd demand tighter quorum and operational hygiene.

Good fit for efficiency

Redis-backed lease/lock patterns are often enough when duplicate execution is acceptable and worst-case impact is wasted compute.

Good fit for correctness

etcd or ZooKeeper are usually easier to reason about for correctness-sensitive ownership, especially when paired with fencing-token enforcement at storage boundaries.

What people often get wrong

  • Declaring "correctness" from lock acquisition alone.
  • Ignoring unlock ownership checks.
  • Assuming a low-latency primitive implies safe ownership under partition and pause behavior.
  • Using Redlock-style claims without matching them to business failure impact.
