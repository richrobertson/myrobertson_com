Distributed Locks vs. Leases vs. Leader Election

These are related coordination tools, but they are not interchangeable. Confusing them causes real operational incidents.

Start here: distributed lock overview.

Definitions

Distributed lock: ownership claim for a critical section. "Only one owner now" is the core promise.

Lease: a lock with bounded lifetime and renewal behavior. Time is part of the contract.

Leader election: chooses a coordinator for a group or partition. Usually implies broader responsibilities than one critical section.

What each gives you (and does not)

  • Lock: mutual exclusion intent. Does not automatically stop stale owners from writing.
  • Lease: eventual owner turnover after crash. Does not kill paused processes.
  • Leader election: a current control authority. Does not by itself define write-path safety for every resource.

Operational consequences

Locks and leases fail mostly at ownership handoff boundaries. Leader election fails mostly when systems assume leadership means global correctness for all side effects. In practice, you still need idempotency, fencing, and explicit ownership checks where state mutates.

Comparison table

  • Ownership semantics: lock/lease are per lock key; leader election is per group/term.
  • Expiry/renewal: explicit for leases; implicit term changes for elections; optional/implementation-specific for locks.
  • Failure handling: leases depend on renewal and expiry authority; election depends on quorum and term monotonicity.
  • Typical use cases: cron ownership (lock/lease), maintenance leadership (leader election), control-plane authority (leader election + lease contracts).
  • Risks: stale writers, split-brain assumptions, and accidental broadening of authority semantics.

Examples engineers actually run into

Cron ownership: a lease-backed lock is usually enough if duplicate execution is acceptable.

Maintenance leadership: one node should run compaction at a time; use election semantics and stop work immediately on leadership loss.

Control-plane coordination: election selects current coordinator, but per-resource writes still need token/version validation.
